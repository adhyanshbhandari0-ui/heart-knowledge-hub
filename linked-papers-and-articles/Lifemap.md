The UK looks different when you map healthy years, not just life expectancy.

Life expectancy tells us how long people live. Healthy life expectancy tells us how long they live well.

The biggest healthy-life gap is inside the country.

In England, women living in the most deprived 10% of small areas have a healthy life expectancy 20.3 years shorter than women in the least deprived 10% (2022–2024).
That is wider than the gap between any two English upper-tier councils for women (19.1 years). 
The gap is wider than in 2013–2015, when it was 19.1 years. (ONS, published 15 April 2026.)

Source: ONS — Healthy life expectancy by national area deprivation, England (15 April 2026) · Wales . England uses 10 IMD deciles; Wales uses 5 WIMD quintiles. Period: 2022–2024.

Note: the deprivation banding does not come from a single index across the ONS time series (2011–13 to 2015–17 use IMD 2015; 2016–18 to 2021–23 use IMD 2019; 2022–24 uses IMD 2025). Areas can therefore change band between periods because of index revisions as well as real change.

Life expectancy is familiar. Healthy life expectancy is more revealing.

It estimates how long people can expect to live in good general health. Across the UK's 216 council areas with published figures, healthy life expectancy differs by 22.6 years between the highest and the lowest.

Healthy life expectancy (average of men and women, 2022–2024) ranges from 50.9 years in Merthyr Tydfil to 73.5 years in Orkney Islands. Estimates for councils with small populations have wide margins of error.

Across London's 32 boroughs with published figures, healthy life expectancy ranges from 58.0 years in Greenwich to 69.8 in Richmond upon Thames: a spread of 11.8 years within one city.

Adult smoking, weight, physical activity, diet and drinking also vary widely between English councils: part of the backdrop to the differences in healthy years. Bars show the lowest to highest council figure.
