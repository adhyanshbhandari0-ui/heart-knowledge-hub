[November 3, 2026] In their July 23 Science article, " Single-cell multiomics connects 3D genome and transcriptome alterations in Alzheimer’s disease ", Zhang and coworkers examine perturbations in 3-dimensional structure of chromatin in nuclei of the prefrontal cortex of individuals with Alzheimer's Disease (AD) that may cause previously observed perturbations in gene expression in AD. Using a new technique, GAGE-seq, that measures gene expression and the proximity of genes to one another in the same cell, as well as a technique that measures accessibility of the chromatin, they find that active and inactive genes become increasingly intermingled in Alzheimer's Disease, and the influence of nearby transcription control elements becomes reduced while the influence of more distal elements is enhanced. Analysis of Reactome pathway gene sets shows that this intermingling is associated with upregulation of mitochondrial function, notably respiratory electron transport , and with decreased transmission across chemical synapses and signaling by receptor tyrosine kinases . Knowledge of these effects may help guide the future development of therapeutics.

Reactome is a free, open-source, curated and peer-reviewed pathway database. Our goal is to provide intuitive bioinformatics tools for the visualization, interpretation and analysis of pathway knowledge to support basic research, genome analysis, modeling, systems biology and education.

The development of Reactome is supported by a grant from the US National Institutes of Health (U24 HG012198).
